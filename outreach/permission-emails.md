What we ask for is deliberately small: **the short descriptor words** a supplier already
publishes for a material ("woody, amber, velvety"), not marketing copy. What we need from a
"yes" is a written grant, from someone entitled to give it, under one of two licences:

- **CC0** (preferred): the words join the corpus with no conditions, like the patent rows.
- **CC BY 4.0** (fallback): the words ship in a separately labelled layer that credits the source.

Any "yes" goes into the corpus as its own provenance class (`source: licensed by X`), never
mixed with the patent-derived rows. Contact addresses are not filled in — use each company's
official general, press or R&D/open-innovation contact, not a personal address found online.

handles product information or intellectual property. I'm happy to send the dataset

manufacturers.

Dear De Hekserij team,

I'm building OpenScent, an open dataset linking fragrance molecules to odour descriptors,

Your product pages describe many of them. Would you allow the short descriptor words from

A short reply saying which licence you grant is all I need. Thank you either way. Your
catalogue has already been a great help.

Kind regards,
